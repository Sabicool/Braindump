Removing access to distraction works better than trying to tolerate boredom

5 September 2026·Sabiqul Hoque

The obvious thing to say is that we reach for our phones to get away from being bored, which is true but not worth writing down on its own since everyone says it. What’s more interesting is what follows from it, because when I actually tried to fix this the thing that worked wasn’t what I expected.

When I did the dopamine detox my goal was to increase my attention span, on the assumption that the problem was how quickly I got bored. That isn’t what happened. What made the difference was uninstalling the apps, using them only through the browser where the experience is unpleasant enough that I don’t last long, and putting a time limit on the rest with LeechBlock. My tolerance for boredom didn’t obviously improve. The alternative just stopped being available.

If that’s right then reaching for the phone is mostly a question of what’s within reach rather than a disposition I need to train out of myself, and the useful interventions are all environmental. That also matches what I wrote in Boredom can be a source of motivation, where the fix was going to the common room to study rather than getting better at sitting still.

Opposes

Opposes
the standard framing of the dopamine detox, and most of the attention‑span discourse around it, which treats the problem as an over‑stimulated reward system that needs retraining. On that account the detox works by rebuilding tolerance and the abstinence is the mechanism. I tried it that way and got the result through access instead, so either the retraining takes much longer than I gave it or it wasn’t doing the work.

Bounds

Bounds
Distraction‑Free Phone and Do not look at your phone in the morning, both of which are environmental interventions and so are instances of this rather than separate ideas.
Bounds
Eliminate Distractions.

What would change my mind

If I reinstalled the apps now and found my usage stayed low, then something did change in me over those years and the access explanation is insufficient (I could actually run this, though I’m not especially keen to).

The weaker thing that would also count against it is if the effect wore off while access stayed blocked, which would suggest the blocking was only ever buying time.
